Reviewer 1

Foreshadow musical genre analysis a bit more
> The front portion of the paper is relatively abstract and barely makes reference to the genres, which are central to the analysis. It would help a generalist readership understand the results if the type of results we encounter were anticipated more in the front half. Put differently, we learn a fair amount about musical tastes from the results, but have very little preparation for it in the introduction/lit review.

Response: I have revised the introduction and literature review sections to foreshadow the substantive focus on musical genres. I now introduce the specific genres analyzed and anticipate some of the key findings earlier in the text, ensuring that generalist readers are better prepared for the empirical results in the second half of the paper.

Response: The reviewer is definitely right about this, especially in the characterization of the schema as “things old people like.” This schema has an extremely right-skewed age distribution and thus is mostly composed of old people. Following the reviewer’s suggestion with regard to the tripartite distinction implied in the schema, I renamed to “Contempo/Folksy/Easy” and rewrote the relevant paragraph accordingly. 

Clarify the Intuitions Behind the Expected Influence Measure
Response: I have rewritten this section to clearly introduce the "Expected Influence" (EI) measure as weighted degree centrality, citing Barrat et al. (2004). I explicitly attribute the "spreading activation" interpretation to Robinaugh et al. (2016). Following the reviewer's excellent suggestion, I explain the intuition behind spreading activation—where an increase in one node probabilistically causes an increase in positively tied nodes and a decrease in negatively tied nodes—which clarifies why EI is an appropriate measure of "influence."

Response: I have completely rewritten the validation section to make the logic more transparent. As suggested, I now introduce Robinaugh et al.'s simulation study to provide context before moving to the empirical validation. I have simplified the sentence structure, removed excess jargon, and incorporated mathematical notation and clear variable names (e.g., predictiveness, importance) to clearly delineate the steps involved in linking network centrality to behavioral omnivorousness.
